Key Learnings

How Data Moves from Sender to Receiver

Encoding: Data is converted into signals that can travel through a medium.

Transmission: Signals move across cables, fiber, radio waves, or other media.

Control: Protocols manage timing, addressing, errors, flow, and routing.

Security: Cryptography and policies protect data from unauthorized access.

Communication History

Diagram of a Telegraph

The telegraph is an early example of data communication. A sender presses a key to create electrical pulses, the wire carries the signal, and the receiver converts the pulses into clicks that can be interpreted as Morse code.
