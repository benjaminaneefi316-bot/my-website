Topic Notes

Course outline with subtopics

These notes explain what each topic means in data communication and list the subtopics to study before lectures, tutorials, practicals, presentations, and revision.

Week 2Lectures / Tutorial

Networking Fundamentals

Networking fundamentals introduce how computers and devices connect to share data, resources, and services. This topic explains the purpose of networks and the basic language used to describe communication systems.

Subtopics

  • Network devices, nodes, links, clients, servers, and peer-to-peer communication.
  • LAN, MAN, WAN, internet, intranet, bandwidth, throughput, latency, and reliability.
  • IP addresses, MAC addresses, ports, protocols, packets, and basic troubleshooting.
Week 3Lectures / Tutorial

Network and Network Reference Models

Reference models break communication into layers so network tasks are easier to understand, design, and troubleshoot. They show how data is prepared, addressed, transmitted, routed, and delivered to an application.

Subtopics

  • OSI model layers: physical, data link, network, transport, session, presentation, application.
  • TCP/IP model layers and how they compare with the OSI model.
  • Encapsulation, decapsulation, protocols, addressing, routing, and service delivery.
Week 4Lectures / Tutorial

Transmission Media

Transmission media are the physical or wireless paths that carry signals between devices. The chosen medium affects speed, distance, cost, security, and resistance to interference.

Subtopics

  • Guided media: twisted pair cable, coaxial cable, and fiber optic cable.
  • Unguided media: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, and satellite communication.
  • Media selection based on bandwidth, attenuation, noise, cost, installation, and coverage.
Week 5Lectures / Tutorial

Signals

Signals are the electrical, optical, or radio forms used to carry data. Understanding signals helps explain how binary data moves through real communication channels.

Subtopics

  • Analog and digital signals, amplitude, frequency, phase, period, and wavelength.
  • Encoding, modulation, multiplexing, bandwidth, bit rate, and baud rate.
  • Noise, distortion, attenuation, interference, and signal quality improvement.
Week 6Lectures / Tutorial

Wireless Networks and Technologies

Wireless networking allows devices to communicate without physical cables. It supports mobility, flexible access, and wide coverage, but it also needs careful security and interference management.

Subtopics

  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, cellular networks, hotspot access, and wireless access points.
  • Radio frequency, channels, signal strength, coverage, roaming, and interference.
  • Wireless standards, authentication, encryption, and safe public network use.
Week 7Lectures / Tutorial

CIA Triad and Cryptography

The CIA triad explains the three main goals of information security: keeping data private, accurate, and available. Cryptography supports these goals by protecting data while it is stored or transmitted.

Subtopics

  • Confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, authorization, and non-repudiation.
  • Symmetric encryption, asymmetric encryption, public and private keys.
  • Hashing, digital signatures, certificates, SSL/TLS, and secure communication.
Week 8Lectures / Tutorial

Data Security, Network Security and Cybersecurity

This topic focuses on protecting data and systems from unauthorized access, misuse, disruption, and attack. It connects technical controls with responsible user behavior.

Subtopics

  • Malware, phishing, social engineering, denial-of-service attacks, and insider threats.
  • Firewalls, antivirus tools, intrusion detection, VPNs, backups, and patch management.
  • Password safety, multi-factor authentication, access control, monitoring, and incident response.
Week 9Lectures / Tutorial

Review of Data Protection Policies and Acts

Data protection policies and laws guide how personal and organizational information should be collected, used, stored, shared, and deleted. They help protect privacy and build trust in digital systems.

Legislative instruments and industry standard documents

  • Privacy principles, consent, lawful processing, data minimization, accuracy, and retention.
  • Rights of data subjects, breach reporting, accountability, and organizational policies.
  • Examples of protection frameworks, cybersecurity standards, and acceptable use policies.
Week 10Practical

Network Simulation Practical

Network simulation practicals allow students to design and test networks in software before building them physically. They make it easier to see how devices communicate and how configuration choices affect performance.

Subtopics

  • Creating topologies with PCs, switches, routers, servers, and wireless devices.
  • Configuring IP addresses, subnet masks, gateways, routing, and basic services.
  • Testing connectivity with ping, packet tracing, and troubleshooting failed links.
Week 11Presentation

Class Presentation

Class presentations help students explain technical ideas clearly and confidently. A good presentation uses simple language, correct terminology, diagrams, and examples from real communication systems.

Subtopics

  • Choosing a topic, researching credible sources, and organizing main points.
  • Using diagrams, examples, demonstrations, and clear slide structure.
  • Speaking confidently, answering questions, and citing materials properly.
Week 12Interactive Review

Revision

Revision brings all course materials together so students can check understanding, correct weak areas, and prepare for assessment. It is most effective when it combines summaries, practice questions, diagrams, and discussion.

Communication Systems

From local links to global networks

Modern communication systems combine hardware and software. Network interface cards, switches, routers, modems, access points, servers, and satellites all help data move from a source to a destination.

  • LAN: connects devices within a small area such as a room, school, or office.
  • WAN: connects networks across cities, countries, and continents.
  • Internet: a global network of networks built on shared standards.
  • Satellite communication: uses uplink and downlink radio signals through orbiting satellites.

Historical Foundation

Telegraph diagram

The telegraph is one of the earliest electrical communication systems. It used a key, battery, transmission wire, and receiver to send coded pulses over long distances.

What Inspires Me

Communication technology can change access

I am inspired by the way networks make education, business, emergency services, and personal communication possible across distance. Data communication shows that technology is not only about machines; it is about giving people faster access to information, opportunity, and each other.

When a message can travel clearly, securely, and quickly, a person can learn, work, ask for help, and share ideas beyond the limits of their physical location.
